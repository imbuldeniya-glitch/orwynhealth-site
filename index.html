The problem

Musculoskeletal care goes wrong in both directions

Some patients are sent for a surgical opinion they do not need, and wait months to be told so. Others are managed for a year with a problem that needed an operation. First contact clinicians already make these decisions every day. What the system does not consistently do is make the specialist decision thresholds applied downstream, and the eventual outcome of the referral, available at the point of decision.

NHS England calls the work of getting this right MSK referral optimisation, and it is not the same as reducing referrals. We explain the terms in plain English: what MSK clinical decision support is, how MSK triage works, and who first contact practitioners are.

What Orwyn is

A surgeon's criteria, written down and shown

Every suggestion Orwyn makes comes with the reason for it, in plain words. There are no scores and no percentages. The clinician can disagree and record why, and that disagreement is kept.

Used by the clinician

Orwyn is not a patient app and not a questionnaire the patient fills in at home. It sits with the clinician while the patient is in front of them, and it fits inside a normal appointment. This is clinician-facing decision support rather than patient self-triage, and the difference matters.

It shows its reasons

Orwyn runs on rules a named consultant surgeon has written and signed off, built from the strongest research evidence and national guidance. It is not a model whose reasoning cannot be inspected: anyone reviewing a decision can be shown why it was made.

With you, not above you

Orwyn does not check, approve or oversee the clinician's work. It carries a surgeon's judgement into a room the surgeon is not in. The clinician assesses the patient and decides.

In a clinic

Six things happen, and only one of them is a decision

A decision on its own changes very little. What makes a plan hold is that the patient believes it, that the difficult cases still reach a surgeon, and that the clinician finds out what happened.

  1. 1

    The safety questions come first

    Nothing else appears until every safety question has been answered. An unanswered question is never read as a no, and anything that should not wait is named, with where the patient should go and how quickly.

  2. 2

    The decision, with its reasons

    One suggested next step, in plain words, with the reasoning shown and the evidence behind each part of it. The clinician can disagree, and the disagreement is recorded rather than overruled.

  3. 3

    The patient leaves with it

    The plan the clinician has agreed with the patient, in plain English, texted or printed in the room. What was decided, why, and what would change the answer. A decision not to operate arrives as an answer rather than a refusal.

  4. 4

    The hard cases still reach a surgeon

    Some patients cannot be settled by any criteria, and those are usually the ones who most need judgement. Orwyn prepares them for an existing multidisciplinary meeting, and records the decision and who made it. It does not hold the meeting or make the decision.

  5. 5

    The clinician finds out what happened

    Today a referral is made and the outcome goes somewhere else, so the clinician who decided never learns whether they were right. Orwyn sends that outcome back to them. It is theirs alone: never a comparison with colleagues, never a report to a manager.

  6. 6

    And it counts all of it

    Every decision, every disagreement and every outcome is recorded, so a service can show what changed rather than assert it. Nothing about how Orwyn behaves changes by itself: rules change only when a named surgeon changes them, in writing, with a version and a date.
