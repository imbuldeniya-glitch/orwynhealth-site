For insurers and employee health providers

MSK decision support for insurers.

The definition

What an MSK tool for insurers does

For a health insurer, an employee health scheme or an occupational health provider, an MSK tool is useful if it improves one decision above all: whether and when a musculoskeletal case needs a surgeon. Almost everything that follows, the scans, the specialist appointments, the time off work, flows from that decision.

Orwyn gives the clinical team making that decision a consultant surgeon's written judgement, at the moment they make it.

The problem

The decision that drives the journey

Musculoskeletal care often runs in steps: physiotherapy, then a scan, then a specialist. Each step is reasonable on its own. The cost comes when the steps run in the wrong order, in either direction. In the NHS, NHS England reports that almost a third of patients seen at a first trauma and orthopaedic outpatient appointment in 2019/20 were discharged after that first visit.

The other direction costs too, though it is rarely counted: a person who needed a surgeon early spends months in treatment that was never going to work, and returns to work later than they should have.

What Orwyn offers

A surgeon's judgement, written down

Consistent decisions

The same case gets the same reasoning, whichever clinician on the team sees it, because the rules are written, versioned and dated.

Reasons you can audit

Every suggestion shows why, and traces to published evidence, so a decision can be reviewed after the event.

Both directions

Orwyn looks for the case that needs a surgeon sooner as carefully as the case that does not need one at all, and asks about urgent problems first.

Where it stands

Honest about the stage

Orwyn has not yet been used with real patients. It covers the knee first, and a first NHS evaluation is being planned. No results are claimed anywhere on this site.

Most tools built for MSK triage aim to keep people out of hospital. Orwyn helps the clinician decide when a patient should go in, when they should not, and gives the clinician and the patient a reason to trust the answer.
