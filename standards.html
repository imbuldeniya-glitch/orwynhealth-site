The standards

What we build to, and where each one stands

Clinical safety: DCB0129

DCB0129 is the NHS clinical risk management standard for manufacturers of health software. It requires a named Clinical Safety Officer who is a suitably qualified and experienced clinician. That role is held by Dr Arj Imbuldeniya, appointed on 19 August 2026, who is also the author of the clinical rules. The standard permits this and it is usual at our stage, so we state it plainly rather than imply a separation that does not exist.

A hazard log and a clinical risk management plan are in place. The ten knee safety rules are approved by the Clinical Safety Officer for implementation and evaluation, most recently on 8 October 2026. They are not approved for live clinical use. Before Orwyn goes live in any service, the clinical safety case report is completed and the NHS organisation carries out its own assessment under the companion standard, DCB0160, with our support. An independent clinical review is planned before first deployment.

Status: Clinical Safety Officer appointed 19 August 2026; safety rules approved for implementation and evaluation; safety case report in progress; independent review planned, not yet in place.

Medical device regulation

Orwyn is health technology built to the engineering and documentation standards expected of regulated clinical software under UK medical device regulation, with specialist regulatory advice taken before any classification commitment, and the intention to certify.

Status: built to the standard from the first line of code; classification confirmed with regulatory advice before launch.

NHS Digital Technology Assessment Criteria

The DTAC is the NHS's baseline assessment for digital health technologies, covering clinical safety, data protection, technical security, interoperability and usability. Orwyn is being mapped against every DTAC criterion as it is built, so that assessment is a formality rather than a scramble.

Status: mapping in progress.

Data protection and cyber security

UK GDPR by design, with data minimisation as the starting point rather than an afterthought. This website sets no cookies and runs no analytics or tracking. Cyber Essentials certification is planned ahead of first deployment, alongside the NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit.

Status: nothing collected from patients; certifications scheduled before deployment.

Data minimisation

Nothing collected back from the patient

Orwyn collects nothing back from the patient: no app, no login, nothing to fill in. Our intention is that the clinician's record of the consultation is held for the NHS organisation that deploys Orwyn, under its control, in the UK, with access logged. Where and how it is held will be agreed with each deploying organisation before any use.
