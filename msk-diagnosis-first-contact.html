MSK diagnosis at first contact.

The definition

What a first contact diagnosis is

An MSK diagnosis at first contact is the working diagnosis a clinician reaches at the first appointment for a musculoskeletal problem. It comes mostly from the history and the examination, often before any scan, and it decides the first test, the first treatment and whether a specialist is needed.

It is written for GPs, first contact practitioners, MSK physiotherapists and advanced practitioners. It is not written for patients.

Why it is hard

Many problems, one short appointment

Several conditions can look alike in the first minutes, imaging is not always available or helpful at that stage, and the clinician rarely hears what the surgeon later found.

That last point matters most. Without the outcome of each referral coming back, it is hard for anyone to know which of their working diagnoses were right.

How Orwyn helps

The most likely answer, with its reasons

Orwyn asks the safety questions first, then shows the most likely diagnosis with its reasons, the test to request, what to do today, and whether and how urgently a surgeon is needed. It covers the knee first.

Most tools built for MSK triage aim to keep people out of hospital. Orwyn helps the clinician decide when a patient should go in, when they should not, and gives the clinician and the patient a reason to trust the answer.

Orwyn has not yet been used with real patients. It covers the knee first, and a first NHS evaluation is being planned. No results are claimed anywhere on this site.

For the knee in detail, see when knee pain needs an X-ray, when an MRI helps at first contact, suspected ACL injury, the locked knee, the hot swollen knee and knee osteoarthritis at first contact.
