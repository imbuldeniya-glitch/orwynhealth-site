## About the product

1. (Home, lede) Orwyn puts a consultant knee surgeon's written judgement into the first-contact consultation: what is most likely going on, what to check, which test to request, what to do today, and when a surgeon is needed. Every answer shows its reasons, and the clinician decides.
2. (Home and what-orwyn-does, model) Steps one to six work in the demo today. Returning the outcome to the clinician, stored records and connection to the record system are planned.
3. (Walk-through, step 1) The clinician confirms the safety questions before anything else appears, and if a rule applies Orwyn names one clear action, where and how quickly.
4. (Walk-through, step 2) The answer comes first, with the findings behind it, two other diagnoses to keep in mind and the actions to take today.
5. (Walk-through, step 3) The letter to the patient with a box for the GP, the letters to the surgeon and to physiotherapy, and the request to radiology are written, ready to check, copy or print.
6. (Walk-through, step 4, confirmed by you) The clinician agrees or disagrees, and the record keeps both.
7. (What it does) At the end of the appointment the patient is given the plan the clinician has agreed with them, in plain English, printed or shown on screen. [tagged "In the demo"]
8. (What it does) Orwyn is designed to return the outcome to the clinician who made the referral. It will be theirs and theirs alone. [tagged "Planned"]
9. (Evidence) A working demo with example patients, covering a demonstration look-up of example patients by NHS number, the safety questions, the most likely diagnosis with its reasons, tests, what to do today, review and escalation, and the letters. Automated tests run on every change to the software. Returning outcomes to the clinician, stored records and connection to a real record system are planned.
10. (Evidence) Orwyn has not yet been used with real patients.
11. (Home, questions) Is Orwyn artificial intelligence? No. Orwyn runs on rules a named consultant surgeon has written and signed off, built from national guidance and research evidence.
12. (Home, questions) Does Orwyn replace the clinician? No. Orwyn does not check, approve or oversee the clinician's work. The clinician assesses the patient and makes every decision.
13. (Home, strip) Every rule written, versioned and sourced.
14. (See the demo) The full demo is open to clinicians, service leads and commissioners. It uses example patients only. We reply with the access password, usually within one working day.

## The six clinical pages: what they say Orwyn does ("How Orwyn handles this")

15. (X-ray) Orwyn picks the view set from the diagnosis the clinician agrees, writes the request as weight-bearing, and puts the clinical question and the reason on it, with the findings that led to it. For a suspected fracture it also writes the plan after the X-ray.
16. (X-ray) Orwyn asks about weight-bearing at the assessment only, which makes it deliberately broader than the validated Ottawa Knee Rule. You ruled this on 20 September 2026.
17. (MRI) Where the receiving clinic cannot see scans ordered by the service, Orwyn writes the referral without the scan and says why.
18. (Locked knee) When the examination records a fixed block to straightening, the locked knee safety rule stops routine advice and gives one action: urgent referral to the acute knee clinic.
19. (Hot, swollen knee) When Orwyn's hot, swollen knee safety rule sends a patient to hospital, the screen stops and shows one action: A&E at once, for aspiration before antibiotics, the same day. See NEXT_DECISIONS.md item 1: this page leaves out the 48-hour trial, as you instructed.
20. (ACL) One tap records how the injury happened. It is copied into the clinic note, the referral, the physiotherapy referral and the imaging request.
21. (Osteoarthritis) Orwyn suggests the steroid injection only for a patient who is not being referred for joint replacement, and warns against it for a patient who is.

## Evidence, as the clinical pages report it

40. (X-ray) NICE is clear that osteoarthritis can be diagnosed clinically, without imaging, in people aged 45 or over with activity-related joint pain and morning stiffness of 30 minutes or less, and that imaging should not routinely be used to make that diagnosis.
41. (MRI, ACL) The BASK and BOSTAA best practice document sets assessment in an acute knee injury clinic within 2 weeks of presentation, with MRI as the imaging of choice.
42. (Locked knee) The BASK meniscal guideline recommends urgent arthroscopic surgery for a locked knee. It gives no number of hours or days. A retrospective study of 60 bucket-handle tears found that the time to surgery made no difference to reoperation or outcome.
43. (Hot, swollen knee) The five points quoted from the BSR, BOA, RCGP and BSAC hot swollen joint guideline, and the BOA peri-prosthetic joint infection standard.
44. (Osteoarthritis) NICE recommends a topical anti-inflammatory first; an oral anti-inflammatory with a stomach-protecting medicine if that is ineffective or unsuitable; paracetamol is not routinely offered. A steroid injection gives short-term relief, which NICE puts at 2 to 10 weeks. NICE advises against hyaluronan injections.
