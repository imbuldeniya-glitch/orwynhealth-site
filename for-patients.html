For patients

My clinician used Orwyn. What does that mean?

Orwyn is not yet in routine use in the NHS. It is being prepared for evaluation with NHS services. If your clinician used it, this page explains what it is and what it means for you, in plain words.

What is Orwyn?

Orwyn is a tool your clinician uses during your appointment. It sets out a consultant knee surgeon's written guidance on knee problems: what is most likely going on, which tests help, what to do now, and when a surgeon is needed. It shows your clinician the reasons for each suggestion. It is not an app for you, and it does not give advice to you directly.

Who made the decision about my care?

Your clinician did. They listened to you, examined you, weighed what Orwyn suggested and decided with you what should happen next. They can disagree with Orwyn, and when they do, their decision is the one that counts.

What is my letter, and my plan?

Your clinician may have given you a letter or a printed plan written with Orwyn's help and checked by them. It says what they think is most likely going on, what happens next, and when to get seen sooner. The box for your GP tells your GP what, if anything, they need to do.

Who do I contact with a question?

Please contact the service that saw you. Their details are on your letter. Orwyn cannot answer questions about your care, and please do not send health information to Orwyn. If you are worried about your symptoms, contact the service that saw you, your GP or NHS 111. In an emergency, call 999.

How is my information handled?

Orwyn collects nothing from you: there is no app, no account and nothing for you to fill in. The record of your appointment belongs to the NHS organisation that treated you, which is responsible for it. Their privacy notice explains how they use your information, and they are the people to ask about it. Orwyn's own privacy notice covers this website.
