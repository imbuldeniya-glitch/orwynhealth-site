AI and MSK triage, explained.

The definition

What AI MSK triage means

AI MSK triage usually means software that uses machine learning, or a large language model, to suggest how urgent a musculoskeletal problem is, where the patient should go, or what the diagnosis might be. It learns patterns from data rather than following rules a clinician has written.

The phrase is used loosely. Some products labelled AI are mostly questionnaires with fixed rules; some are genuine learning models. It is worth asking which, because the two behave very differently when something goes wrong.

Strengths

Where AI helps

Volume

Learning models can read and sort large numbers of free text messages and forms quickly, which matters where services are swamped.

Patterns

With enough good data, a model can spot combinations of features that a simple checklist would miss.

Language

Language models can turn a patient's own words into structured information, and draft letters and summaries.

Hard questions

Where AI struggles

Three questions are harder for a learning model than for written rules, and they matter most at the point where a patient's care is decided.

Can it explain itself? A model's reasoning can be difficult to show in a form a clinician, an information governance reviewer or a coroner can check.

Who is accountable? When a suggestion is wrong, someone must be able to say which part of the tool was wrong and fix it.

Does it stay the same? A model retrained on new data can change its behaviour in ways that are hard to see, which is why regulators treat change control seriously.

In the UK many of these products are regulated as medical devices; the MHRA guidance on software and AI as a medical device sets out how.

Orwyn's approach

Why Orwyn uses written rules

Orwyn is not artificial intelligence. It runs on rules a named consultant surgeon has written and signed off, each linked to the published evidence behind it, so every suggestion shows its reasons and can be checked.

Rules have limits too: they only know what has been written into them. That is why every clinician's disagreement with Orwyn is recorded and reviewed, and why the rules are versioned and dated like any other clinical document.

Most tools built for MSK triage aim to keep people out of hospital. Orwyn helps the clinician decide when a patient should go in, when they should not, and gives the clinician and the patient a reason to trust the answer.

Orwyn is currently being assessed for an NHS pilot to test its safety, clinical effectiveness and cost effectiveness.
